Strategic Profit Model

Return on assets is the product of two things: how much profit each dollar of sales keeps (margin) and how many dollars of sales each dollar of assets produces (turnover). Supply chain decisions move both.

The Big Idea

Margin × Turnover = Return

Picture two lemonade stands, each worth $100. Stand A sells $500 of lemonade a year and keeps 2 cents of every dollar: $10 of profit. Stand B sells only $100 a year but keeps 10 cents of every dollar: also $10. Both earn a 10% return on the $100 stand, one through speed and one through margin. Discount retailers win like Stand A; specialty retailers win like Stand B.

Where the Supply Chain Fits

Which Decisions Move Which Side

Supply Chain DecisionMoves Margin ByMoves Turnover By
Better Forecasting and ReplenishmentFewer markdowns and stockouts, so gross margin risesLess inventory needed, so assets shrink
Supplier Negotiation and SourcingLower purchase cost, so COGS fallsLonger payment terms free cash, though they do not change total assets directly
Distribution Network DesignLower freight and handling cost in COGS or SG&AFewer or busier facilities, so PP&E works harder
Store Labor and AutomationLower SG&A per dollar of salesAutomation adds PP&E, so turnover can dip until volume grows
Own Versus LeaseRent is in SG&A, depreciation may be separateLeased buildings stay out of PP&E, which raises PP&E turnover and ROPA
